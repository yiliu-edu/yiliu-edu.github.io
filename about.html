    My Story

    As an instructional designer and K–12 educator, I am excited to keep growing as a person and as a professional who shares knowledge and designs learning that is engaging, interactive and effective, and that creates measurable change for the people I serve.

    My lifelong love of learning began with books. Growing up in China, I read everything I could find, and that curiosity carried me through the gaokao to the University of Hong Kong. There I majored in Chinese Language and Literature, with minors in Finance and Science Entrepreneurship, and graduated with First Class Honors. Literature taught me how people make meaning; finance and entrepreneurship taught me how ideas become something real. Pairing a humanist’s eye with a builder’s mindset still shapes how I approach every challenge.

    My love of learning soon became a love of teaching. Over the past several years I have taught learners from elementary school to university, as an all-subject K–12 teacher, an IB Chinese teacher, and a teaching assistant and academic advisor at HKU. In 2018, I co-founded ITI Informatics Academy, a nonprofit that now offers coding courses to more than 20,000 learners a year. At UK Metrotown Academy in Hong Kong, I designed customized lessons across math, science, Mandarin and English, and recruited, onboarded and trained a fifth of the teaching staff. Along the way, I also led localization strategies that helped AI and EdTech start-ups grow their user bases.

    These experiences led me naturally to instructional design. I am now completing an M.S. in Education at Northwestern University and earning an Illinois Professional Educator License with elementary (grades 1–6), ESL, bilingual and Mandarin world language endorsements. That training grounds my design work in how people actually learn. At Digital4Good x #ICANHELP, I lead curriculum for Meet LEO and Meet LEA, Google-partnered train-the-trainer programs on online safety and AI literacy. I use needs analysis and action mapping to find the real performance gap, then turn complex problems into interactive, data-driven learning, from scenario-based practice tools to AI-assisted workflows that keep content current. As a lifelong learner, I keep building my toolkit, including Articulate Rise, Canva and AI tools like Claude.

    Beyond instructional design, I make time for the things that keep me curious and creative: hiking, playing basketball, writing music, cooking and baking, getting lost in a good book, and watching movies, especially horror films, my favorite genre. I also share study and career guides with my 8k followers on RedNote, and I’ve recently started learning the theremin from scratch. Being a beginner again reminds me how it feels to sit on the learner’s side, and helps me design with more empathy for everyone I support.

    AI in my practice

    Artificial intelligence (AI) forms an essential part of my design creation process rather than being a supplementary tool. It helps me turn ideas into tangible prototypes quickly, but I firmly believe in keeping a human in the loop: through prompt engineering, I keep refining the AI’s output until it is actionable and reasonable.

    Build with Claude

    I prototype interactive learning with Claude and code: scenario practice, onboarding paths and simulations that learners can try the same week.

    Design AI workflows

    I write reusable skills and prompts, structure content so AI can work with it, and set review rules for accuracy and accessibility. See Release-Ready Learning Content.

    Teach AI literacy

    I develop Meet LEA, AI literacy training for educators in a Google partnership, and I’ve introduced K–12 students to AI with tools like Teachable Machine.

    Create content faster

    I use Gemini for research and drafts, Canva for visuals and CapCut for short learning videos, then edit everything to my own standard.
