    From Hello to First Pitch

    Case study: an AI-first onboarding sprint that takes new small-business ads sellers from day one to a confident first discovery call, with proficiency bars that show when they’re ready.

    Try the demoPlay it on this pageBranching scenario

    The problem

    New ads sellers usually start with days of product slides. By the time they make their first call, they can list features but can’t run a conversation: they pitch before they understand the business, promise results they can’t measure, and end calls without a next step. Managers can’t tell who is ready until a real client is on the line.

    I wanted onboarding that is short, personal and built around the job itself: learn only what a seller needs to explain on day one, practice the call before it matters, and use clear bars so “ready” means the same thing for every new hire.

    Business goal and action map

    Goal (Action Mapping): new sellers run their first qualified discovery call by day 10, and their first calls end with a dated next step.

    Business goal

    First qualified pitch by day 10

    What sellers must do
    • Ask about the client’s goal before recommending
    • Match a small, measurable campaign to that goal
    • Answer a concern without discounting
    • Leave with a dated next step
    • Check the AI-drafted call note before saving
    Practice in the demo
    • Day 2: plan the call with the job aid
    • Day 3: AI client role-play with a coach
    • Day 3: check the AI-drafted call note
    • Day 4: five scenario decisions, no hints
    • Day 7: spaced recall
    Minimum information
    • Four essentials cards
    • Call planner
    • Call stages

    Gilbert’s Behavior Engineering Model

    A slow ramp isn’t only a knowledge gap. Training covers one cell; the job aid, the bars and the manager routine cover the rest.

    #CellWhat gets in the wayHow it’s addressedFixed by
    1InformationNew sellers don’t know what “ready” looks likePublished bars for each milestone and a ramp review their manager also seesProcess
    2ResourcesNothing to use during a live callCall planner by client type: questions, likely concerns, a starting point, a success metricJob aid
    3IncentivesEarly calls are judged on closing, so sellers pitch too soonFirst-call rating on the call stages and a dated next step (first-call rubric)Process
    4Knowledge and skillsAd concepts and discovery skills are newMicrolearning cards, AI role-play with coaching, scenario checkTraining
    5CapacityToo much content in week onePre-check marks topics to skip; four short cards instead of a product deckDesign
    6MotivesFear of looking unprepared in front of a real clientSafe practice with an AI client, unlimited replays, feedback on every moveDesign

    Objectives and evidence

    Planned backward: each objective has matching evidence in the sprint and a bar before any content was written.

    #Objective (audience, behavior, condition, degree)Evidence in the demo
    1Given a client’s question, a new seller will choose the accurate explanation of how search ads are ranked, targeted, judged for quality or measured, on the first try and without the card, for at least 3 of 4 topics.Day 1: pre-check (tested-out topics count) and checks with the card hidden
    2In a simulated discovery call, a new seller will ask about the client’s goal before recommending, address a concern without discounting, and end with a dated next step, finishing with client trust of 70 or more.Day 3: AI client role-play
    3Given an AI-drafted note from the call, a new seller will find the missing agreed detail before saving.Day 3: CRM note check
    4Without hints, a new seller will choose the best response in first-month client scenarios for at least 4 of 5 items drawn from an item bank.Day 4: scenario check

    Design decisions

    Test out, don’t sit through

    A four-question pre-check marks each topic “tested out” or “learn”. Tested-out topics count as done, so experienced hires spend their time on what they don’t know yet (Knowles: experience and self-direction).

    Retrieval, not rereading

    Each card ends with “Test me”: the card hides and the seller answers from memory. A wrong answer sends them back to the card, not to the answer.

    Earn the next step

    Each day unlocks when the one before it is done, so nobody reaches the client call without the essentials or the call plan. Locked steps say what’s needed to open them.

    Practice before it matters

    The AI client reacts to every choice, trust rises or falls, and a coach names the move. Weak choices are part of the practice: the seller sees the consequence and tries again.

    AI drafts, the seller checks

    After the call, AI drafts the CRM note but leaves out the success metric. The seller has to find it before saving, the same habit they need with any AI tool at work.

    Spaced and fair

    The scenario check draws 5 items from a bank of 8, so a retry tests the same objectives with new situations. A Day 7 recall brings back the topics the seller found hardest.

    Try the sprint yourself

    You play a new seller: take the pre-check, learn the essentials, prep a call, talk to Rosa the bakery owner, and pass the scenario check. Progress is saved in your browser.

    The call as a branching scenario

    Day 3 also works as a standalone branching scenario, built on the slide model used in Articulate Storyline 360: a player with a menu and seekbar, timed entrances, a character with Normal, Happy and Skeptical states, feedback layers, a Trust variable and conditional results. It’s built in HTML; the build guide maps every slide, layer, state, variable and trigger to Storyline for an LMS version.

    How I use AI to build it

    Generative AI speeds up the drafting; a person decides what new hires see. I wrote two reusable prompts for this project.

    #StepAI doesA person checks
    1Scenario itemsDrafts realistic client moments with three options and an explanationRemoves trivia, rewrites weak distractors, confirms every ad concept with a product expert
    2Client personaPlays Rosa in a live role-play and adds a coach note after each seller messagePersona stays within her profile; coach notes teach one call stage each
    3Demo branchesPersona transcripts become the scripted choices in this demoEach weaker option is something real new sellers say, with a reason; the best answer moves between A, B and C
    4Call notesDrafts a CRM note from the callThe seller checks it before saving
    5Rosa’s voiceKokoro, an open-source text-to-speech model, voices each of Rosa’s linesI listen to every clip and regenerate any line that sounds off

    The demo uses scripted branches so it runs anywhere without an API key. The persona prompt is ready for a live AI role-play.

    Keeping content current

    Ad products change often, so every asset has an owner, a version and a review date. When a product changes, the owner checks every asset that mentions it, the same way my release-ready content workflow does.

    AssetOwnerVersionReview

    AI client persona and coach notesLearning designer1.0After each cohort, using call results
    Scenario item bank (8)Learning designer, with a product expert1.0After each cohort, using item results

    Evaluation plan (Kirkpatrick)

    #LevelMeasureHow and whenPlanned target

    2LearningFirst-try essentials, call trust, note check, scenario score, spaced recallDemo data, days 1–7All bars met
    3BehaviorManager rating of the first live call on the same call stages; calls ending with a dated next stepShadowed call on day 10, rated with the first-call rubricDated next step on most first calls
    4ResultsDays to first qualified pitch; days to first closed dealSales system, days 10–60, compared with the previous cohortFaster ramp than the previous cohort

    Targets are planning targets for a real rollout, not results. Item and call data from each cohort feed the next version: weak items are rewritten and coach notes are updated.

    What I changed after a framework review

    After the first version, I checked the sprint against each framework I use and fixed what didn’t hold up.

    FrameworkWhat I foundWhat I changed
    Gilbert’s BEMThe incentive fix (rate first calls on stages, not closing) had no tool behind itWrote the first-call rubric for managers, using the same call stages as the practice
    Action MappingChecking AI output is part of the job, but the sprint only showed the noteAdded a note check: the draft leaves out the agreed metric and the seller has to find it
    Backward design and ABCDObjective 1 said “explain”, but the evidence is a choice; the bar counted retries as correctRewrote it as “choose the accurate explanation … on the first try”, and the bar now counts first tries only
    KnowlesThe pre-check marked topics “skip”, but sellers still had to answer their checksTested-out topics now count as done; locked steps say why they’re locked
    Retrieval practiceChecks sat under visible cards, so sellers could read the answer“Test me” hides the card; a Day 7 recall brings back the hardest topics
    KirkpatrickLevel 1 was planned but never collectedConfidence is rated on Day 1 and Day 5, and planner usefulness on Day 5, inside the sprint
