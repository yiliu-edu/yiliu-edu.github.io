    At the Pond

    Case study: a first-grade Mandarin–English digital lesson and the production system behind it: storyboard, metadata, vendor guidelines and bilingual quality checks.

    Try the demoPlay it on this page

    The problem

    Dual literacy programs ask young students to build reading skills in two languages at once. Digital lessons can help, with audio for every word and instant feedback, but only if every screen is right in both languages. One wrong tone mark or mismatched audio file teaches the wrong thing to thousands of students.

    Errors like these usually come from the production process, not the content: unclear storyboards, vendors without language-specific rules, and QC that checks one language more carefully than the other. As a bilingual educator, I designed the lesson and the system that keeps it accurate.

    Goal and action map

    Goal: Every lesson screen ships correct in both languages, and students make explicit connections between the languages they’re learning.

    Objectives and evidence

    Planned backward: each objective has matching evidence in the demo.

    #Objective (audience, behavior, condition, degree)Evidence in the demo
    1Given a four-page bilingual book, a first grader will read and listen to every page in at least one language.Read and listen
    2Given four pictures, a first grader will match each to its word in Mandarin or English.Word match
    3Given “The duck swims / 鸭子游泳”, a first grader will identify the word that changes in English but not in Chinese (CCSS L.1.1c).Language comparison
    4Given a vendor build and the storyboard, a designer will flag every issue in both languages with no false flags.QC exercise

    Design decisions

    Both languages, one screen

    “Both” mode shows the sentences together, so students can lean on the stronger language.

    Audio for every word

    Tapping a single word plays it, which supports decoding in both languages.

    Make the bridge explicit

    Color-coded who and action show the shared sentence pattern; the question targets the one difference.

    Color plus labels

    Color is never the only signal, so the comparison works for students who can’t see color.

    Storyboard as the contract

    Every row lists text, pinyin, audio file, interaction, alt text and feedback in both languages.

    QC that mirrors real errors

    The vendor build includes the errors that happen most: tone marks, audio mapping, alt text and feedback language.

    Try it yourself

    First play the lesson as a student, then switch to the designer’s view and QC the vendor build. Progress is saved in your browser.
