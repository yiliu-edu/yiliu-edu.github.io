    PolicyDesk Go-Live

    Case study: a blended training program for a policy-servicing system rollout at an insurance brokerage, with work samples in four modalities: web-based training, virtual and in-person instructor-led sessions, and job aids.

    Try the simulationWork samplesDownload the PDF packet

    The problem

    Northgate Insurance Brokers, a fictional brokerage with 140 account managers in six offices, is moving policy servicing from email, a team spreadsheet and an old policy system into a new platform, PolicyDesk. Policy changes are the task people do most often, and every slow or wrong change reaches a client.

    Operations asked for “system training for everyone.” Talks with operations managers and underwriting narrowed it down: about one change request in four comes back for missing information, people pick the wrong client when names look alike, and staff worry the new system will slow them down during renewal season.

    Purpose

    The purpose of this program is twofold: first, to have 90% of account managers pass the simulation test at 80% or higher before go-live; and second, within 30 days of go-live, to cut the share of change requests returned for missing information from about 25% to under 10%, and the average time to submit a change from about 20 minutes to under 8. A ten-minute simulation, a 90-minute virtual practice session, a half-day lab for floor champions and a two-page desk card serve account managers, assistants and team leads in every office.

    Goal and action map

    Goal: Every policy change is submitted in PolicyDesk, complete the first time, from go-live day.

    Objectives and evidence

    #Objective (audience, behavior, condition, degree)Evidence
    1Given a client email, an account manager will submit a complete policy change in PolicyDesk, scoring at least 80% of steps correct with no hints.WBT: Test it round, reported to the LMS
    2Given three requests with built-in problems, an account manager will spot the issue before submitting in at least 2 of 3.vILT: breakout scenarios A to C, checker notes
    3Given a colleague stuck on a request, a floor champion will guide them to submit it without touching their keyboard.ILT: coaching station, observer checklist
    4Given an issue, a floor champion will route it to the right place: fix it, IT help desk or underwriting, in at least 18 of 20 cards.ILT: triage card sort and skills check

    Choosing the modality

    Each part of the program uses the format that fits the job it has to do and the people it has to reach.

    WhenModalityWhoWhy this format
    3 weeks before go-liveWBT, 10 minutesAll 140 account managers and assistantsThe eight steps are the same for everyone, so self-paced practice scales and sets a shared baseline
    2 weeks beforevILT, 90 minutesRemote offices, up to 18 per sessionExceptions need discussion and live practice; a virtual session reaches six offices with no travel
    1 week beforeILT, half day12 floor championsCoaching and triage need role-play and observation in the room
    Go-live and afterJob aid, 2 pagesEveryone, at the deskSupport at the moment of need, when the real request arrives

    Design decisions

    Watch, try, test

    The simulation demonstrates the task once, then lets learners practice with hints, then checks them on a new client with no hints.

    Real-looking requests

    Every activity starts from a client email, so learners practice reading the request as well as clicking through screens.

    Exceptions live

    The vILT session skips the basics the simulation already covered and spends its time on the cases that send requests back.

    Roles in breakout rooms

    Driver, navigator and checker keep all three people working and make the checker habit visible.

    Champions coach with questions

    The ILT lab trains champions to ask before they show, so colleagues finish each request themselves.

    One card, two sides

    The job aid shows the eight steps with screenshots on one side and where old tasks moved on the other.

    WBT: try the simulation

    You play an account manager submitting a policy change. Watch the demo, try it with hints, then handle a new client email with no hints. Built in HTML on the Storyline 360 model and published as a SCORM 2004 package.
